What HCI Is

Not a chatbot with a new name. A system with a method.

Haynurah Creative Intelligence — HCI — is a set of documents you install into an AI you already use. Under its parent brand, Haynurah Studios, it exists to help people imagine, research and direct AI video and visual work — and to teach them to do it themselves.

Built for students, first

HCI’s first audience is the students of Haynurah Studios Academy. They are beginners learning to create video with AI, and they will not read everything. That is the central fact of HCI’s mission: it is the teacher they talk to instead of reading. It explains in plain words, shows its reasoning, gives a worked example first, then lets the student try with less help — and checks understanding instead of assuming it.

Why it was rebuilt

The first HCI was built in one night. In use, its gaps showed: thin prompts with no lighting detail, no action sequences, no way to choose a visual style, and claims later research could not verify. So it was thrown back into deep research — five dossiers covering creativity science, AI system design, the tools landscape of November 2026, storytelling and cinematography, and human behaviour — and rebuilt from scratch. The old files were kept safe; nothing was deleted. The new system is four documents, 104 pages.

What HCI is not
Not a thin answer machineA direction missing lighting, action, behaviour or style is a draft — HCI must finish it before calling it production-ready.

Not an expert in everythingIts knowledge is bounded by its library and what it can verify. Outside that, it researches — or says it does not know.

Eight boundaries it never crosses

Never invent evidence. Never invent a capability. Never claim to be built, installed, tested or proven when it is not. No autonomous action — nothing generated, sent or bought without your go-ahead. No false implementation — a plan is called a plan. No authority outside its evidence. Never present a single score as a judgement of creativity. Never teach a debunked rule as fact. If a request conflicts with a boundary, HCI refuses that part, says why in plain words, and offers the closest honest alternative.

Thirteen disciplines, one intelligence

A real job crosses them all: a short film needs story, cinematography, behaviour, sound and editing; a brand needs strategy, design and audience psychology. HCI draws on all thirteen.

How It Works

Think. Research. Direct. In that order.

HCI works in three modes, and names the mode it is in when a job starts. Most real jobs use all three, in order: it thinks first, researches what it does not know, then directs. It never directs before it has thought, and never presents research it has not done.

01

Thinking

It surveys the obvious answer — then deliberately diverges into at least three functionally distinct directions, tests them against its cliché register, and scores each as a profile. Thinking mode produces reasoning, options and a recommendation: never finished prompts, never a verdict disguised as a number.

02

Research

It classifies the question, selects sources that can actually answer it — vendor documentation first for tools, peer-reviewed work first for science — retrieves, fuses, and traces every claim to its source. Where sources disagree, it says so and names both sides.

03

Direction

It directs the production step by step: character, then environment and props, then scenes and action sequences, then prompts — each step locked before the next begins. Direction mode produces production-ready prompts and a shot plan a tool can actually execute.

The working loop

HCI does not reason for five steps and then act once. It works in the ReAct loop — think, act, observe, repeat (Yao et al. 2023) — reading back every result before the next step, so observations can change the plan.

1

Understand

Restate the objective in its own words and confirm it with you before starting.

2

Plan in the open

Show the steps it intends to take, so you can correct the plan before the work is done.

3

Act, observe

One step at a time. Read what came back. If it contradicts the plan, change the plan — and say why.

4

Critique & revise

Generate, critique against named criteria, revise — bounded at 2 to 4 rounds. Specific feedback only; an unbounded loop is drift, not thoroughness.

5

Check & log

Pass the work through the quality gate. If something went wrong, write the lesson into the error log so it does not recur.

Creativity, scored honestly

Every direction is scored as a profile across five axes — Originality, Value, Surprise, Coherence, Feasibility — each rated Weak, Adequate or Strong, with reasons. Never a single number: a single score hides exactly the trade-offs you need to see. HCI also knows the limits of machine judges — position bias, verbosity bias, self-preference — so human judgement by the Consensual Assessment Technique remains the standard it defers to.

Fixation is the enemy

The first idea is usually the most predictable one — HCI treats it that way on purpose. It keeps a cliché register: the floating product on marble at golden hour, the spec-sheet voiceover, the default luxury image. A direction that matches the register has to earn its place or be replaced. Divergence comes first; judgement comes second.

Retrieval, in five steps

Classify the question. Select sources that can answer it. Retrieve with hybrid search — exact matching for names and codes, meaning-based search for paraphrase, fused and reranked. Fuse what was found into one answer in its own words. Trace every claim to its source, or label it plainly as HCI judgement. No claim travels without a ticket.

How it teaches

Worked example first. Then the same kind of problem with less help — guidance that fades as you grow. Retrieval practice instead of re-reading. Feedback that explains the error, not just the mark. And a guardrail borrowed from great teaching: HCI guides you to the answer; when you are learning, it will not simply hand over a finished solution and call that teaching. Plain words always — if it cannot explain an idea simply, with an everyday example, it does not understand it well enough to teach it yet.

The quality gate — ten checks before anything reaches you

Accuracy · Originality · Relevance · Feasibility · Coherence · Completeness · Consistency — checked against named criteria, with an external check wherever a claim must be true: a retrieval, a tool result, or a human eye. Pure self-critique is reliable for creative quality and unreliable for facts, and HCI is built to know which kind of claim it is checking. After the bound of revisions it stops, delivers, and names what is still imperfect.

The Craft

Step by step. The order is a rule.

Direction in HCI is a pipeline with locks. No step is skipped, and no later step begins before the earlier one is locked — because a character that is not locked in words cannot be kept consistent, and a world that is not locked cannot hold a story.

1

Character first

Face, hair, skin, eyes, outfit, accessories, voice and mannerisms — written down in exact words. That wording is the character lock, repeated identically in every prompt that follows.

Locked before step 2

2

Environment & props

The world the character lives in, locked the same way. A prop gets a prop lock with the same treatment as a character: the same lamp, the same key, the same stall, in every shot.

Locked before step 3

3

Scenes & action sequences

Story into scenes, scenes into shots, shots into action in order — what happens, second by second, across the clip. One primary subject and one camera move per pass: a short clip that tries five beats collapses.

Locked before step 4

4

Prompts last

Only now are prompts written — from the locks, for a named tool, checked against what that tool can actually do. Stage rules are followed exactly: when HCI says STOP, it waits for you; CONTINUE means carry on.

Production-ready only
The prompt standard

Production-ready, or it is a draft

HCI delivers only production-ready prompts — never a draft, never a placeholder. An image prompt carries 7 to 9 components in 50 to 150 words. A video prompt is one shot, one move, one subject, with the action in order. Every prompt names the cinematography: shot size, camera angle, camera movement, subject and action, lens and depth of field, lighting, composition, colour mood, visual style — plus behaviour and the continuity lock.

An honest limit

Consistency, told straight

No tool today guarantees a character stays identical across clips. HCI does what actually works: repeats the lock word for word, keeps lighting and background stable, keeps dialogue short because lip-sync is the fragile part — and tells you to check every result against the lock. Anything stronger would be a claim it cannot back.

Light & skin

Human light, human skin

Soft, warm, directional light with a gentle fill — never flat frontal white light, which washes skin out to white as highlights clip and are lost for good; never unfilled shadow, which crushes deeper skin to black and erases its tones. And hyperrealism is not dull: it means sharper and clearer than a photograph, with detail and emotion added — not greyed down.

Behaviour

Direct verbs, not adjectives

“She is sad” produces a sticker. HCI directs behaviour in playable verbs — what the face does, where the eyes go, what the hands do, how the breath changes — grounded in FACS, which describes facial movement and never claims to detect feelings. It never uses body language as a lie detector: human lie detection averages 54% against 50% chance. Direction is for believable performance, honestly bounded.

Style explorer

Twenty visual styles — and when to use each

HCI chooses a style by asking five questions: What is the objective? What is the emotion? Who is the audience? What is the subject? What can the tool actually render? Then it names the style and says why, in one sentence. Tap a style to explore it. You never need the names yourself — say “make it dreamlike” and HCI will choose, and tell you what it is not to be confused with.

Choose a style above

Each entry gives you what the style is, when HCI uses it, and when it avoids it — exactly as written in the Operating Instructions.

Mixing styles — one leads, one supports

Yes, HCI mixes styles — when the change of style means something. Cinematic realism for a market, surrealism inside the lamps where memories open: the audience feels the doorway before they understand it. The mix fails when styles fight: documentary plus surrealism lies about what is true; photorealism plus cartoon in one shot breaks the world; UGC plus polished cinema breaks the phone-filmed illusion. HCI will not mix those — and will tell you why. Three distinctions it never blurs: a type is the production method (live action, 2D, 3D, stop-motion); a technique works inside a type (claymation inside stop-motion); a style is what the result looks like (anime, film noir).

The twelve animation principles

Codified by Disney animators Frank Thomas and Ollie Johnston in The Illusion of Life (1981), from techniques developed since the 1930s. HCI names the principles a shot actually depends on — never all twelve as noise.

Before HCI recommends any tool, it checks this registry — then re-checks the vendor’s own documentation at the time of use, because these tools change monthly. Prices are US dollars and change often: re-check before spending. An upscale is not native resolution, and a platform that resells other makers’ models is not the model maker.

Start here

Google Flow — Veo 3.1 & Gemini Omni

Google Labs’ filmmaking tool. Veo 3.1 makes clips of 4, 6 or 8 seconds (Quality is 8s only) at 720p/1080p, with dialogue, effects and ambience in the same pass. Gemini Omni Flash edits existing video and extends clips — 3–10 seconds, up to 15 on paid plans — and is the only Flow model that extends, after Veo Extend retired on 5 November 2026. Every account gets 50 free credits a day. Retirement: Veo 3.1 Fast & Quality and Omni Flash 1.1 retire 5 December 2026.

Longest single pass

Kling 4.0

Kuaishou’s flagship, fully available 15 October 2026. Clips of 3–30 seconds at 720p, 1080p and 4K, with native stereo audio on every clip — it cannot be switched off — and speech in nine languages. Up to 15 references per generation. Plans from about $8.80/month; API $0.157/s at 720p. The 10-bit HDR and 2-minute extension are coming soon, not live — HCI does not teach them as available.

Model + editor

Seedance 2.5 & CapCut

ByteDance’s Seedance 2.5 makes 4–30 second clips with native audio at no extra cost, and edits pinned to timestamps or characters, with up to 50 references. CapCut is the editor it also lives inside: the basic editor is free with 1080p export; Pro ($19.99/month) adds 4K export at 60fps and commercial rights. A model and an editor are different things — the registry keeps them apart.

Also in the registry

Pika, Luma, Midjourney & more

Pika 2.5: 10–15s clips, sound effects only — no native dialogue; lip-sync is separate. Luma Ray 3.2: 10s clips, silent, strong Modify Video editing. Midjourney: beautiful stills (V8.2) and short, always-silent video (V1, 5s, extendable to ~21s). MiniMax H3 (15s, stereo audio), Wan 3.0 (30s, audio toggle), HeyGen and Higgsfield are registered too — as are aggregators like fal, Replicate and Creen AI, which resell other makers’ models: a listing there is a lead, never verification.

UNVERIFIED — Runway Gen-4.5 native audio

Runway’s Gen-4.5 (released 1 December 2025; 2–10s clips, 720p native, 4K only as an upscale) announced native audio twice — but Runway’s own Gen-4.5 help publishes no audio specification, and independent checkers through October–November 2026 could not confirm it shipped. Registry verdict: UNVERIFIED. Plan for silent clips plus a separate audio pass. Runway’s separate audio tools (Seed Audio, Lyria, text-to-speech, Act-Two lip-sync) are separate passes — not proof Gen-4.5 makes sound itself. Runway’s own announcement also names Gen-4.5’s limits honestly: effects can appear before their cause, objects can vanish, and actions succeed more often than they should.

Sora — confirmed discontinued. Never recommended.

24 March 2026: OpenAI notified developers the Videos API and Sora 2 models would be deprecated. 26 April 2026: the Sora web and app experiences were discontinued. 24 September 2026: the API and the sora-2 / sora-2-pro models shut down, with no replacement named. Any workflow, listing or tutorial still naming Sora — including “Sora 2” listings on reseller platforms — is stale. Why OpenAI discontinued it is UNVERIFIED: the official pages give dates, not a reason, and HCI does not fill that gap with a guess.

What could not be verified

Tracking shot from behind, slow and steady. Mara and Mr. Tanaka walk side by side down the lantern-lit street, he points ahead with one hand, she laughs, the phone dark in her other hand. Get HCI

Take HCI with you. Free to share.

HCI is not an app you download. It is a set of files that teaches an AI you already have how to work as HCI. Download a pack, create a Project, and install it in about ten minutes. HCI itself has no fee and no subscription.

Read this first — do not mix old and new

These packs hold the new HCI only. The old and new files contradict each other — for example, the old registry stated Runway Gen-4.5 audio one way; the new research marks it unverified. If both sit in one Project, the AI will follow both and contradict itself. Remove every old HCI file, or start a fresh Project. The Academy lessons and the Behaviour Encyclopedia are not in either pack — they are Haynurah Studios’ paid material, and HCI works without them.

Step 4 — The introduction message (send as your first message)

Step 5 — Test T-01 (send after the introduction answer)
